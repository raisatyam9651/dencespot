The basics, plainly

What Is a Hair Transplant?

A hair transplant moves hair you already have into the areas where it has thinned. Follicles are taken from the back and sides of the scalp — the donor area — because that hair is generally resistant to the hormonal process behind pattern hair loss.

Those follicles are separated under magnification and placed individually into the thinning or bald zones, at the angle and direction the surrounding hair already grows. Once healed, they behave like the donor hair they came from: they are washed, cut and styled normally.

Two things decide whether the result looks natural. The first is hairline design — position, shape and irregularity, judged against your face and your age rather than a template. The second is honest arithmetic: the donor area is a finite supply, so coverage has to be prioritised over your lifetime, not spent in one session.

That is why nothing is quoted before an examination. Two people with the same photograph can need very different plans.

In one line

It redistributes your own permanent hair. It does not create new hair — which is why donor supply, not marketing, sets the limit on what is achievable.

Candidacy

Who May Consider a Hair Transplant?

These are the patterns most often assessed for surgery. Being on this list is not the same as being suitable for it.

Receding hairline

The frontal line has moved back and the temples have opened up. Often the most rewarding area to restore, because it frames the face.

Pattern hair loss

A recognisable, staged pattern that has stabilised. Active or fast-progressing loss is usually treated medically first.

Crown thinning

The crown consumes grafts quickly because of its whorl pattern, so it is planned carefully against donor supply and future loss.

Reduced hair density

Hair is present but thinner and finer. Density work is sometimes better served by medical treatment than by surgery.

Hairline restoration & repair

Correcting a hairline that sits too low, too straight, or was placed poorly elsewhere. Repair work needs its own plan.

Selected beard restoration cases

Patchy or scarred beard areas, using the same grafting principles. Assessed on donor supply and the pattern of the gaps.

Suitability depends on individual clinical assessment, hair-loss pattern, donor availability and other factors. Some patients are advised to start with medical treatment, and some are advised not to proceed at all — that recommendation is part of an honest consultation.

The number that actually matters

Techniques

Hair Transplant Techniques

No technique is universally better. The right one depends on your donor area, the area to be covered and the density you are aiming for.

Offered here

FUE Hair Transplant

Follicular units are extracted individually from the donor area with a fine punch, sorted under magnification, then placed into recipient sites prepared in the planned direction. No donor strip is removed.

Considerations: needs adequate donor density; donor area is trimmed; session length scales with graft numbers.

Key benefits: no linear scar, flexible graft distribution, suits most pattern-loss cases.

Learn more about FUE →
Offered here

DHI Hair Transplant

Extraction is the same as FUE; placement differs. Each graft is loaded into a fine implanter and set in one action, which gives close control of depth, angle and spacing.

Considerations: slower per graft, so it suits defined areas rather than very large sessions; shaving requirements are discussed at consultation.

Key benefits: precise angle control for hairlines, temples and detail zones; short time out of the body for each graft.

Learn more about DHI →
Selected cases

FUT Hair Transplant

A donor strip is removed and dissected into grafts, which can yield a high graft number in a single session. Considered only where it is genuinely the better option for that donor area.

Considerations: leaves a linear donor scar; needs longer hair to conceal it; healing differs from FUE.

Key benefits: efficient graft yield in advanced loss where large coverage is the priority.

At a glance

Donor scar

FUE and DHI leave small dot healing spread across the donor area. FUT leaves one linear scar.

Best-suited use

FUE for broad coverage, DHI for hairline and detail zones, FUT where graft volume is the deciding factor.

What stays the same

Local anaesthesia, day-case procedure, growth from roughly 3–4 months, and the same aftercare discipline.

The procedure

How Does a Hair Transplant Work?

Six stages, in order. Nothing about the day should come as a surprise — you will have walked through all of it at the consultation.

01

Consultation

What is bothering you and what you want changed, alongside medical history, medication and family pattern. Photographs are taken for your record.

02

Planning

Donor area evaluated, hairline drawn on your face, grafts allocated zone by zone. You approve the design before anything is scheduled.

03

Preparation

The donor and recipient areas are prepared according to the planned technique, and local anaesthesia is given. The hairline is confirmed one last time.

04

Extraction

Follicles are harvested using the selected technique, then checked and sorted under magnification. Grafts are kept handled as little and as briefly as possible.

05

Implantation

Grafts are placed into the planned recipient areas at the angle, direction and density agreed — single hairs at the leading edge, heavier units behind them.

06

Recovery

You leave with medication, written aftercare and your review dates. The first wash is guided, in person or over video.

On the day

Sessions run through the day with breaks. You are awake and comfortable throughout, and you go home the same day.

Recovery & results

What Generally Happens After Treatment

A general guide to the phases most patients describe. Your own timeline is set by your doctor at your reviews.

Phase 1

Initial recovery

Tenderness, mild swelling and small crusts in the treated areas. Washing starts as instructed, and desk work usually resumes within a few days.

Phase 2

Early shedding

Transplanted hairs commonly shed while the follicles stay in place. This is expected, not a failed procedure — it is the single most misunderstood phase.

Phase 3

New growth

Fine new hairs typically appear from around three to four months, unevenly at first. Texture improves as they mature.

Phase 4

Progressive density

Coverage continues to build through the middle months. Progress is documented photographically at each review.

Phase 5

Longer-term result

A fuller picture around ten to twelve months. Non-transplanted hair can keep thinning, so medical treatment often continues alongside.

Individual recovery and hair-growth outcomes vary. Your doctor will provide personalized guidance based on your treatment, and timelines here are general patient education rather than a promise of results.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers are general patient education. Anything specific to your case is answered at the assessment.

Ask on WhatsApp
What is a hair transplant?+

A procedure that moves your own follicles from a donor area — usually the back and sides of the scalp — into thinning or bald areas, at the angle and direction the surrounding hair grows. It redistributes existing hair rather than creating new hair.

Who is a good candidate?+

Typically someone with a stable, defined pattern of loss, adequate donor density and realistic expectations. Active or rapidly progressing loss, certain scalp conditions and some medical histories mean medical treatment comes first, or that surgery is deferred. Candidacy is decided after examination.

Is a hair transplant painful?+

The local anaesthetic injections are the uncomfortable part; the procedure itself is not typically painful. Mild soreness, tightness or swelling for a few days afterwards is normal and managed with prescribed medication. Pain tolerance varies between patients.

How long does the procedure take?+

A typical session runs four to eight hours as a day case, depending on the graft count. Sessions of around 1,000 to 1,500 grafts sit at the shorter end, while 3,000 or more grafts may run a full day or be planned across two sittings. You go home the same day with written aftercare instructions.

How long does recovery take?+

Most patients doing desk-based work return within three to five days, once the initial swelling settles. Visible crusting usually clears in seven to ten days. Gym work, swimming, direct sun and anything that causes heavy sweating are avoided for around three to four weeks, and your doctor confirms the timings at your review.

When does transplanted hair start growing?+

Transplanted hairs usually shed within two to six weeks while the follicles remain in place, which is expected and not a failed procedure. Fine new growth typically appears from around three to four months, thickens through months six to nine, and reaches a fuller picture at roughly ten to twelve months. Crown areas often run a little slower than the hairline.

Is a hair transplant permanent?+

Transplanted follicles are taken from the occipital donor area, which is genetically resistant to DHT, so they typically continue growing for life. However, your non-transplanted hair can keep thinning, which is why medical treatment usually continues alongside surgery. Without it, a transplanted hairline can end up framing an area that has thinned further behind it.

How many grafts might I need?+

Graft numbers follow your Norwood stage and donor density. As a general guide, Norwood 2 needs about 800 to 1,200 grafts, Norwood 3 about 1,500 to 2,200, Norwood 4 about 2,200 to 3,000, Norwood 5 about 3,000 to 3,800, and Norwood 6 about 3,500 to 4,500 grafts, often across two sessions. The final number is counted from a mapped plan, not estimated over the phone.

What is the difference between FUE and DHI?+

Extraction is essentially the same. The difference is placement: in DHI each graft is set with a fine implanter in a single action, giving close control of depth and angle — useful in hairlines and detail zones. FUE placement into pre-made sites is efficient over larger areas. Neither is universally better.

How much does a hair transplant cost in Gurgaon?+

In Gurgaon a hair transplant generally falls between ₹45,000 and ₹2,50,000, because cost follows the graft count your case actually needs rather than a fixed package price. A Norwood 2 hairline of roughly 1,000 grafts sits near the lower end; a Norwood 5 to 6 case needing 3,000 or more grafts sits at the upper end. DenceSpot issues an itemised written estimate after the assessment, with nothing added afterwards.

Can women undergo hair transplantation?+

Sometimes. Female hair loss is more often diffuse, and hormonal, thyroid, iron or post-partum causes need investigating and treating first. Many women do better with medical treatment; surgery is considered selectively, for suitable patterns such as a receding frontal line.

Can beard hair be transplanted?+

Beard areas can be restored using scalp grafts placed at the correct angle and density for the face. The reverse — using beard hair as a donor source for the scalp — is considered only in selected cases and is assessed individually.

What happens during the consultation?+

History and medication review, scalp and donor examination, pattern staging, photographs for your record, and a written plan covering technique, approximate graft number, timeline and cost factors. Bring your medical history and a list of current medicines.

Is a hair transplant safe?+

Performed by a qualified doctor in a proper clinical setting, a hair transplant is a low-risk day procedure done under local anaesthetic. The usual risks are swelling, temporary numbness, folliculitis and, uncommonly, poor graft survival or shock loss of surrounding hair. Risk rises sharply when extraction and placement are delegated to technicians rather than performed by a doctor.

Does a hair transplant leave scars?+

FUE and DHI leave tiny dot scars across the donor area that are generally not visible once hair grows to a short length, though very close shaving can reveal them. FUT leaves a single linear scar along the donor strip, which is why it is used selectively and always discussed openly beforehand. Healing quality varies between individuals.

Your follow-up reviews are with her as well. The clinic operates from a single address in Sector 39, Gurugram, where both consultations and procedures take place.

What should I ask before choosing a hair transplant clinic in Gurgaon?+

Ask who physically performs the extraction and placement, and whether that person is a doctor. Ask how the graft number was arrived at and to see it mapped. Ask to see unedited before-and-after photographs taken at the same angle and lighting, at ten to twelve months. Ask what the written estimate excludes. A clinic that answers all four plainly is usually the safer choice.
