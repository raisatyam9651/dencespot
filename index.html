Clinical approach

Technology Meets Medical Precision

Equipment does not decide the outcome — how it is used does. These are the parts of the process where technique measurably affects graft survival and how natural the result looks.

In use

Magnification-assisted grafting

Grafts are separated and placed under magnification, matching the angle and direction of surrounding hair.

Why it matters: reduces graft handling damage and is what makes the hairline read as natural.

In use

Direct implanter (DHI)

A fine implanter places the graft in one action, controlling depth and angle in dense or detail zones.

Why it matters: shortens the time grafts spend outside the body in the areas that need the most precision.

FAQs

Ask on WhatsApp
What exactly is a hair transplant?+

Follicles from an area that is genetically resistant to hair loss — usually the back and sides of the scalp — are moved to thinning or bald areas. It redistributes your own hair; it does not create new hair, which is why donor supply governs what is achievable.

Who is a good candidate?+

Typically someone with a stable, defined pattern of loss, adequate donor density and realistic expectations. Active or rapidly progressing loss, certain scalp conditions and some medical histories mean treatment should be medical first, or deferred. Candidacy is decided after examination, not from a photograph.

How is the procedure performed, and how long does it take?+

Under local anaesthesia, as a day-case procedure: the hairline is confirmed, grafts are extracted from the donor area, sorted under magnification, then placed one by one. Session length depends on graft numbers — larger sessions can run most of the day, with breaks.

Is it painful?+

The anaesthetic injections are the uncomfortable part; the procedure itself is not typically painful. Mild soreness, tightness or swelling for a few days afterwards is normal and is managed with prescribed medication. Pain tolerance varies.

How long is recovery, and when do results appear?+

Most people return to desk work within a few days, following the aftercare instructions on washing, sun and exercise. Transplanted hairs commonly shed in the first weeks — this is expected. New growth usually begins around 3–4 months, with the fuller picture at roughly 10–12 months.

Is a hair transplant permanent?+

Transplanted follicles keep the characteristics of the donor area and generally persist. However, non-transplanted hair around them can continue to thin with age, which is why medical treatment and a long-term plan matter. No clinic can guarantee a specific density or a permanent outcome.

What is the difference between FUE, DHI and FUT?+

FUE extracts individual follicular units from the donor area. DHI is a placement method that implants each graft directly with a fine implanter, giving close control of angle and depth. FUT removes a donor strip and can yield high graft numbers in one session, at the cost of a linear scar. The right choice depends on your donor area and the area to be covered.

How many grafts will I need?+

It depends on the area to be covered, the density aimed for and what the donor area can safely give. It is mapped zone by zone at the assessment. Any number offered before an examination is not a plan.

What affects the cost?+

Graft numbers, technique, complexity of the case, number of sessions and any supporting medical treatment. You receive an itemised estimate after the assessment.

Can women have a hair transplant?+

Sometimes — but female hair loss is more often diffuse, and hormonal, thyroid, iron or post-partum causes must be investigated and treated first. Many women do better with medical treatment. Surgery is considered selectively, for suitable patterns such as a receding frontal line.

Can beard or eyebrow hair be restored?+

Yes, using the same principles: scalp grafts placed at the correct angle and density for the face. Beard and eyebrow work is design-sensitive and usually needs fine, single-hair grafts.

What happens at the first consultation?+

History and medication review, scalp and donor examination, pattern staging, photographs for your record, and a written plan with technique, approximate graft number, timeline and cost factors. Bring your medical history and a list of current medicines.
