Safety & standards

The protocols we follow, in the words we would use to a doctor

Ask us anything about safety

Single-use where it matters

Punches, blades, needles and syringes are single-patient, single-use and disposed of as biomedical waste. Reusable instruments are cleaned and sterilised between every patient.

Anaesthesia by the doctor

Local anaesthesia is administered by the treating doctor, with dose recorded against your weight and history. Your medication list and allergies are reviewed before the first injection.

Written, informed consent

Technique, expected graft numbers, risks, shedding phase and realistic timelines are set out in writing and signed before the day — not on the chair, minutes before starting.

Photography & records consent

Clinical photographs are taken at every stage for your record. Publishing any of them anywhere requires separate, specific, written consent that you can withdraw at any time.

S06 Trustworthiness Stated limits and refusals. On a YMYL page, publishing what you will not do outperforms any number of superlatives — and it is the one thing competitors will not copy.

How we advise

Sometimes the honest answer is “not yet”, or “not at all”

Donor supply is finite and spends only once. These are the positions we hold even when they cost us the case.

We defer

When loss is still active

Operating into a pattern that is still progressing produces a result that looks wrong in five years. If your loss is not stable, we treat medically first and review — even if you came in ready to book.

We investigate

Before treating diffuse loss

Thyroid, iron, hormonal and post-partum causes are investigated and corrected first — particularly in women, where diffuse thinning is more often medical than surgical.

We decline

Hairlines that cannot age well

A hairline placed too low or too dense for the donor area looks good in photographs at month twelve and wrong at year fifteen. We design for the face you will have at sixty.

What we will never do

  • Quote a graft number or a price before examining your scalp
  • Guarantee a specific density, or call any result permanent
  • Run same-day discounts or deadlines to push a decision
  • Publish a before-and-after that is not the patient's own, unretouched photograph

What you leave the consultation with

  • Your pattern stage and donor assessment, written down
  • Technique, approximate graft number and why that technique
  • An itemised cost estimate and what would change it
  • A timeline to month twelve, including the shedding phase

Our photography rules
